\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Outdoor navigation in mountain terrain is difficult because \Gls{gnss} signals weaken behind ridges. This project builds a camera-based location estimator for the Khumbu region of Nepal. A reference database of 1.34 million horizon profiles is precomputed from a 30\,m \Gls{dem}. A \Gls{unet} with MobileNetV3-Large encoder segments sky from terrain. The extracted skyline is matched against the database using three scorers fused by Reciprocal-Rank Fusion.

On 300 synthetic queries, 86.4\% of answered queries are localised within 500\,m (median 135\,m). On 68 Google Street View panoramas --- which are low-resolution and frequently affected by haze and fog in the Khumbu region --- a consensus filter selecting high-confidence wide-FOV matches achieves 85.7\% accuracy within 1\,km (median $\sim$40\,m), without any satellite signal.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} skyline matching, terrain localisation, \Gls{unet} segmentation, horizon profile, reciprocal-rank fusion, Khumbu
\end{abstract}
